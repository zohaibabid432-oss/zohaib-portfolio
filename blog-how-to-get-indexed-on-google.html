How to Get Your Website Indexed on Google

By Zohaib Abid, SEO Expert in Bahawalpur, Pakistan · Updated Nov 8, 2026 · 2 min read

Quick answer

To get a page indexed, make sure it can be crawled and is not set to noindex, add it to your sitemap, link to it from other pages, then request indexing with the URL Inspection tool in Google Search Console. Indexing can take from a few days to a few weeks.

How do you request indexing in Google Search Console?

  1. Open Google Search Console and select your property.
  2. Paste the page address into the URL Inspection bar.
  3. Review whether the URL is on Google.
  4. If it is not, click Request indexing.

What should you check before requesting indexing?

  • The page returns a normal 200 status and loads correctly.
  • There is no noindex tag and robots.txt does not block it.
  • The canonical tag points to the same page.
  • The page is in your sitemap and linked from other pages.
  • The content is original and useful, not thin or duplicated.

What does Crawled - currently not indexed mean?

Google fetched the page but chose not to add it to the index yet. This often points to thin, duplicate or low-value content. Improve the page, add internal links to it, then request indexing again.

What does Discovered - currently not indexed mean?

Google knows the URL but has not crawled it yet. Stronger internal links and a clear sitemap help, and a new or slow site may simply need time.

How long does indexing take?

It can take days to a few weeks. Requesting indexing does not guarantee it, so fix quality and linking issues rather than repeating the request.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my website not showing on Google?

Usually the pages are not indexed yet, are blocked by noindex or robots.txt, or are too new. Check the Pages report in Search Console first.

Do I need to submit a sitemap?

It is not required, but it helps Google discover your pages and is easy to submit in Search Console.
